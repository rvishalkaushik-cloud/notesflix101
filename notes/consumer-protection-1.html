❓ Why might an individual consumer find it difficult to challenge a large seller alone?

An individual consumer usually has limited information, money and bargaining power, and no group support, so a large and organised seller is hard to challenge alone. Consumer protection and collective action help balance this.

Who counts as a consumer?

Consumer, as per the Act

A consumer is a person who buys goods or hires or avails services for a consideration. It also includes a person who uses such goods or services with the approval of the buyer, where applicable.

  • Consideration may be paid, promised, partly paid or deferred.
  • Transactions may be offline or online.
  • Not a consumer: buying goods for resale or for a commercial purpose (and likewise availing services for a commercial purpose).
  • Exception: goods bought and used exclusively to earn a livelihood by self-employment are not treated as commercial purpose.
  • Free services are not covered.
REMEMBER: The key idea is purchase or use for a consideration. Always check purpose and facts. A person is not a consumer in every product or service situation.

Case file: Aarav's new phone

Aarav buys a smartphone for personal use from a retailer. After the purchase he finds that the phone repeatedly switches off during normal use. The retailer and the manufacturer have not yet been contacted.
(Illustrative case.)
Aarav may have received a defective product. Consumer protection safeguards buyers against defective products and unfair practices.
  1. Yes. He bought goods for personal use, for a consideration, so he appears to fall within the basic meaning of a consumer.
  2. Goods-related. A smartphone is a good.
  3. Consumer protection is relevant because a defective product affects the buyer's interests, and the law exists to safeguard such interests.
