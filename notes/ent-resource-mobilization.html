Where it starts

Two places every rupee can come from

Before chasing outside investors, every entrepreneur first weighs what they already have against what they'll need to borrow or raise.

Internal sources

Click to expand ↕

The owner's own money — known as equity. For most small entrepreneurs this is a modest amount, which is exactly why an overwhelming share of funding ends up coming from outside.

External sources

Click to expand ↕

Capital markets, angel investors, venture capital and specialised institutions — each carrying its own obligations, responsibilities and opportunities, and each suited to a different stage of the business.

How venture money arrives

The four stages of venture capital

A genuine sequence — each stage has to earn the next.

Seed capital

A small amount to test the idea and prove it's worth building at all.

Start-up finance

The business plan gets built out and a management team takes shape.

Second-round financing

The product is out and selling — funds now go toward winning market share.

Bridge / pre-public

The final push toward an IPO, so early investors can exit with a return.

Who keeps the system honest and funded

One regulator, and institutions at two levels

Click a pillar to expand it.

SEBI

India's securities regulator

  • Set up in 1988, given statutory power in 1992
  • Protects investors and approves stock exchange rules
  • Can inspect, penalise and even prosecute violations

National-level institutions

All-India development banks

  • IDBI — apex institution for term finance to industry
  • SIDBI — dedicated to small-scale industry
  • NABARD — agriculture and rural development
  • IFCI, ICICI, IIBI — sector and project lending

State-level institutions

Region-specific financing

  • State Financial Corporations (SFCs) — state-level term loans
  • TFCI — financing for the tourism sector
  • SIDC — state industrial development
